---
title: Taking the Leap
date: 2021-09-12
excerpt: "Four years on the safe path, and I've never been more sure I need to step off it."
---

For about a month now I've been living two lives, and I've started to notice which one I look forward to.

In the first one I'm an Enterprise Architect at PwC. My days are enterprise integrations, Salesforce talking to MuleSoft talking to some system nobody has touched since the day it was installed, working out which tools a company actually needs out of the pile it has bought over the years, and the technology side of mergers and divestitures, where two companies become one or one becomes two and somebody has to figure out where everything goes. I work with offshore dev teams across time zones, I sit in a lot of calls, and honestly, I'm good at it. It's a good job. It's the safe path, the one people back home nod at when you describe it, and I've been walking it since January 2018.

In the second life it's late at night, the work laptop is closed, and I'm building Dehidden.

I didn't get into crypto because someone pitched it to me. I went down the rabbit hole on my own, the way I go down every rabbit hole, by pulling on one thread and finding it doesn't end. First it was curiosity about how any of it worked, then it was reading everything I could find, then it was the slow realisation that people were laying down new rails for the internet in real time and I was watching it happen from the side of the road. At Syracuse I majored in AI and game theory, and game theory is really just the study of what happens when you change the incentives. Web3 is a giant, messy, live experiment in exactly that. Who owns things, who gets rewarded for showing up early, what happens when a community holds the keys instead of a company. I couldn't stop thinking about it.

So why now, and why not wait until it's all figured out?

Because by then it'll be figured out, and someone else will have figured it out. Frontiers don't stay frontiers for long. Right now the tools are rough, the standards are half-written, most of the people building are doing it because they genuinely believe in it, and the space is small enough that one person with an idea can actually change the shape of something. That window closes. I'd rather be in the room early, making mistakes, than show up in five years and read about it.

There's also a quieter reason. Four years of enterprise work taught me something I didn't expect to value this much, which is how big companies actually buy and adopt technology, and how many things have to talk to each other before anything real ships. Brands are going to want to be part of this new internet (Web3 and metaverses lol), and most of them won't have a clue where to start. The architecture muscle I built at PwC isn't wasted here. It might be the whole point.

I'm not going to pretend I have it all mapped out. I didn't know a thing about running a business, but I had grit and a mission. That's still roughly the state of things. I don't know how to price a product, I don't know how to hire, and I'm learning what a company even is by building one. But I've spent enough years watching projects move at the speed of the slowest approval to know how badly I want to move at the speed of an idea.

The fear is real, I won't lie about that. The safe path is safe for a reason, and walking away from it feels a bit like stepping off a moving train to go explore a forest nobody's mapped. People have asked me, kindly, whether I'm sure. I'm not sure. I don't think you ever get to be sure about the things worth doing. What I am sure about is how I feel at the end of each of these two days. One leaves me tired. The other leaves me tired and happy.

I've always believed life is a game and the point is to keep playing, and to play the kind of game where the people around you win too. This one feels like that. The rules are still being written, the board is still being built, and I get to help build it.

I haven't worked out the timing of everything else yet. But I know which life I'm waking up for.

Time to jump.
