---
title: Building in the Cold
date: 2022-11-27
excerpt: "When the market stops pretending, you find out which parts of your work were real."
---

Honestly, I haven't known what to say about the last few weeks, so I've said very little.

When FTX went down at the start of this month, it felt less like a market event and more like a betrayal that everyone in the room had half-suspected and nobody had wanted to name. One of the biggest, most trusted names in the industry, the one on stadiums and in every panel, turned out to be doing the exact thing this whole technology was supposed to make impossible. People who'd been told to trust the big friendly exchange lost what they'd put in. People outside crypto, who've put up with my enthusiasm for over a year, have every reason to roll their eyes at me now. I understand why.

It's cold out here now. Winter came in stages this year, and this is the part where you stop pretending it's just a bad week.

The strange thing is that I feel calmer than I did at the peak.

At the peak, everything was loud. Every brand wanted an NFT, every announcement got attention, every roadmap sounded plausible because the market was rewarding plausible. We were busy in a way that felt like success, and some of it was.

But I'd be lying if I said I didn't sometimes feel a gap back then, between how much the world was willing to pay for what we did and how much what we did was actually worth to the people using it. In a bull market, those two numbers drift apart and nobody complains, because the drift feels like momentum. It was easy to look at the demand and assume it meant value. Some of it did. Some of it was the tide.

Winter closes that gap, whether you want it to or not. Speculation leaves first, and what's left is the question I keep saying brands should ask themselves: are people here because of the thing, or because of the moment? I've been asking that about our own work these last few weeks, and I don't love every answer.

I believed in self-custody before any of this, and FTX is the loudest possible argument for it. The people who held their own keys still have what's theirs. A lot of the people who trusted a company to hold them don't. That's the whole point we've been making, and it's grim to watch it get proven this way. It doesn't make me want to leave. If anything it reminds me why I came. The ideas I care about, owning your stuff, positive-sum networks, communities that actually share in what they build, didn't collapse this month. A company that ignored them did.

So what does building look like when it's cold?

For me, right now, it looks like quieter work. Listening harder to which things people kept using after the airdrop, after the announcement, after the hype cycle moved on. Being more honest with clients about what's worth doing and what's just noise, even when saying so costs us a deal. Being honest with myself about what we're really good at, and what we were only being paid for because the market was generous.

I don't have a neat plan. What I've got is a feeling that the next year is going to be about value in the plainest sense of the word, what's actually useful to someone, and that anything built on less than that won't last the season.

It's cold. We're still here. Build things that are worth something when nobody's cheering, and the seasons stop mattering.
