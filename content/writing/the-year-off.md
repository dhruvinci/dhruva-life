---
title: The Year Off
date: 2024-01-21
excerpt: "I'm taking a break from work. I am not taking a break from obsessing."
---

"So what's next?"

I've been asked this more times in the last month than in my whole life put together. At first I'd fumble through an answer about exploring options and taking some time, and I could see people nodding politely the way you nod at someone who's clearly lost. So now I just say it plainly. I'm taking the year off.

Then comes the second question, usually with a slightly worried face. "And doing what?"

Jiu-jitsu. Mostly jiu-jitsu.

Let me explain, because I know how it sounds.

I haven't really stopped in years. I loved every bit of it and I'd do it again. But somewhere in there I stopped asking what I wanted to do and only asked what needed to be done next, and those are very different questions.

So when the last chapter closed at the end of the year, I made myself a promise. I'd take a proper career break. Not a two-week trip and then back to pitch decks. A real one.

The thing is, I know myself. I can't do nothing. I've tried, and it lasts about four days before I start inventing projects nobody asked for. Even on a break I have to obsess over something, and if I'm going to obsess, I want it to be something meaningful. Something that'll still be paying me back when I'm sixty.

When I sat with that honestly, two things came up, and they turned out to be the same thing.

The first was my health. Running a startup is not kind to your body. You sit too much, you sleep too little, you eat whatever's on the table at 1am, and you tell yourself you'll fix it after the next launch. There's always a next launch. I want to give my body the same attention I gave my company for two years, and see what happens.

The second was learning. I think the thing I'll miss most about building isn't the launches or the revenue, it's the feeling of being a beginner at something hard and slowly, painfully, getting less bad at it. I wanted a sport that works like that. Not one where you peak at twenty-five and then spend the rest of your life talking about it, but one that's basically a lifelong puzzle.

Jiu-jitsu is both. I've trained on and off before, always squeezed around work, always a bit guilty about the time. This year there's nothing to squeeze it around. I'm going all in - six hours a day, six days a week, here in Bangalore.

Yes, six. Yes, I'm aware that's a lot (my body has already filed a formal complaint, and it's only January).

People have asked if I'm worried about falling behind. Honestly, a little. The world moves fast and a year is a long time to step off. But I think falling behind is only a problem if you're in a race, and I'm not sure I ever was. I'm more interested in the long game. A year of learning how to use my body well, how to stay calm under pressure, how to lose a hundred times a week and come back the next morning - I have a feeling that'll pay off in ways I can't predict yet.

And if it doesn't, I'll have spent a year getting healthier and doing something I love with good people. I can live with that.

Nothing would make me happier than going to bed exhausted every day, knowing I gave it my all.

See you on the mat.
