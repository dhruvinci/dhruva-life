I'm Dhruva. I take the things I love and turn them into things I build - and lately, into research that checks whether those things actually work.

Right now that's machines and human movement. I wanted a training partner that could watch my jiu-jitsu rolls and tell me what really happened, so I built one - [kakashi.ai](/work/kakashi) - and then spent months finding out exactly where the models fall apart. That became my first paper, [accepted at CAISc 2026](/research/faithful-movement). These days I'm trying to get fast and slow visual thinking to work together: small models and CV tools that react, VLMs that reason. Vision first, and down the line, robots that can understand and work with people. There's a decade of work in there I want to sink my teeth into.

Before this I was the founding AI engineer at [Zopu.ai](/work/zopu), where we turned drawings into working products for people who don't write prompts, and grew to about 200,000 daily users. Before that I founded [Dehidden](/work/dehidden), building Web3 products for brands like Adidas x Prada and Mercedes, until Polygon acquired us. And before that I spent four years in the US at [PwC](/work/pwc) as an enterprise architect.

In 2024 I took a year off and trained jiu-jitsu six hours a day, six days a week. Competed twice. Even on a break I need something to obsess over, and I wanted it to be my health and something I could keep learning for the rest of my life.

If there's a pattern, it's that the things I love don't stay hobbies for long. Jiu-jitsu became Kakashi and a paper. Music became [pushit.tv](/work/pushit), and a list of [265 bands I've seen live](/music) that keeps growing. I sow seeds and play long games.

I'm open to research and open to work - [say hi](/contact).
